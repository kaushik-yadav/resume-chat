# Resume writing best practices

## Strong bullet structure
<!-- tags: bullets, action verbs, impact, enhancement -->
A strong bullet follows "Action verb + what you did + how + measurable result". Start every bullet with a past-tense action verb for previous roles and present tense for the current role. Lead with the outcome when it is impressive ("Cut checkout latency 40% by ..."). Keep bullets to one or two lines. Avoid weak openers such as "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped with", "Participated in", "Duties included" - they describe tasks instead of impact.

## Action verbs by theme
<!-- tags: action verbs, enhancement, wording -->
Leadership: led, directed, spearheaded, mentored, coached, championed, mobilized.
Building: built, engineered, architected, designed, developed, launched, shipped, implemented.
Improvement: optimized, streamlined, accelerated, reduced, automated, modernized, refactored, consolidated.
Analysis: analyzed, modeled, forecasted, evaluated, quantified, diagnosed, investigated.
Collaboration: partnered, collaborated, aligned, negotiated, facilitated, influenced.
Growth: increased, expanded, grew, scaled, generated, delivered.
Use each verb at most twice across the resume to keep the language varied.

## Quantifying impact honestly
<!-- tags: metrics, quantification, numbers, honesty, enhancement -->
Numbers make impact concrete: scale (users, requests per second, data volume, team size), money (revenue, cost saved), time (hours saved, latency reduced, release frequency) and quality (error rate, uptime, test coverage). Never invent figures. When the original text has no number, either keep the claim qualitative or insert a clearly marked placeholder such as "[add metric: % latency reduction]" so the candidate can fill in the true value. Approximate ranges ("~30%", "10+ services") are acceptable only when the candidate provided them.

## ATS (applicant tracking system) friendliness
<!-- tags: ats, keywords, formatting, job description -->
Use standard section headings (Summary, Experience, Education, Skills, Projects, Certifications). Mirror exact keywords from the job description where they truthfully apply (e.g. "scikit-learn" rather than only "ML libraries"). Spell out acronyms once ("Natural Language Processing (NLP)"). Avoid tables, text boxes, headers/footers and images for critical content - many ATS parsers skip them. Keep dates in a consistent format.

## Consistency rules across the document
<!-- tags: consistency, formatting, tense, enhancement -->
Use one date format everywhere (e.g. "Jan 2022 - Present"). Use present tense only for the current role. End bullets consistently (all with or all without periods). Keep job-title lines in the same order (Title, Company, Location | Dates). Use the same spelling for technologies throughout (PostgreSQL vs Postgres). Avoid first-person pronouns ("I", "my") in bullets.

## Summary section
<!-- tags: summary, profile, positioning -->
A summary is 2-4 lines: target role and seniority, years of experience, 2-3 core strengths with the most relevant technologies, and one signature achievement. Tailor it to the target role or company. Avoid clichés ("hard-working team player", "results-driven") unless backed by evidence.

## Skills section
<!-- tags: skills, keywords, role targeting -->
Group skills by category (Languages, ML/Data, Frameworks, Cloud & Tools). Put the skills most relevant to the target role first. Only list skills the candidate can discuss in an interview; do not add skills that do not appear anywhere in the candidate's experience - instead list them as skill gaps to work on. Remove outdated or trivial items (e.g. "Microsoft Word") for technical roles.

## Tailoring to a company
<!-- tags: company, culture, values, tailoring -->
Tailoring means re-prioritizing and re-wording true experience to reflect what the company values: e.g. scale and technical depth for large tech companies, ownership and speed for startups, reliability and compliance for finance and healthcare. Reflect company vocabulary only where it truthfully fits (e.g. "customer obsession" stories for Amazon, "user-first" for Google). Never claim employment, products or achievements the candidate did not have.
