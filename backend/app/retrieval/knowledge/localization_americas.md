# Hiring conventions: the Americas

## United States (en-US)
<!-- tags: en-US, united states, usa, america, resume -->
Document name: "resume". Length: one page for under ~10 years of experience, two pages for senior candidates. Reverse-chronological order. Do not include a photo, date of birth, age, marital status, religion or national origin - US anti-discrimination norms mean recruiters often reject resumes that include them. Letter paper size. Dates as "Jan 2022 - Present". A short summary is optional; skills and impact-focused bullets matter most. US spelling (optimize, organization).

## Canada (en-CA / fr-CA)
<!-- tags: en-CA, fr-CA, canada, quebec -->
Very close to US conventions: no photo, no personal data such as age or marital status, one to two pages. Canadian spelling mixes British and American forms (e.g. "colour", "organize"). In Quebec, French-language resumes are expected for many roles; bilingual ability (English/French) is a strong differentiator and should be stated with levels.

## Mexico (es-MX)
<!-- tags: es-MX, mexico, mexican, spanish, latam, latin america -->
Document name: "currículum vitae" or "CV". Length: one to two pages. Section headings commonly used: "Perfil profesional", "Experiencia profesional", "Formación académica", "Habilidades" or "Competencias técnicas", "Idiomas", "Certificaciones". A professional photo is still fairly common in Mexico but is optional and increasingly omitted at multinational and tech companies; never add one on the candidate's behalf - mention it as an optional choice. Traditional CVs sometimes list age or marital status; modern practice (especially in tech) omits them, so do not add personal data the candidate did not provide. List language levels explicitly, e.g. "Inglés: avanzado (C1)" - English proficiency is highly valued by Mexican employers. Use Mexican Spanish vocabulary: "computadora" (not "ordenador"), "celular" (not "móvil"), "licenciatura" for a bachelor's degree (e.g. "Licenciatura en Ciencias de la Computación" or "Ingeniería en Sistemas Computacionales"), "maestría" for a master's. Dates are commonly written "ene. 2022 - actualidad" or "enero 2022 - presente". Write bullets in impersonal past tense or infinitive-noun style ("Desarrollé...", "Lideré...", or "Desarrollo de..."), and keep a formal, professional register. Do not translate company names, product names or technology names (Python, AWS, PostgreSQL). Mention willingness to relocate or work location (e.g. "Ciudad de México") when relevant. Salary expectations and the RFC/CURP identifiers do not belong on the CV.

## Brazil (pt-BR)
<!-- tags: pt-BR, brazil, brazilian, portuguese, latam -->
Document name: "currículo". One to two pages. Common headings: "Objetivo", "Resumo profissional", "Experiência profissional", "Formação acadêmica", "Competências", "Idiomas". An "Objetivo" line stating the target role is common. Photos are not expected. State language levels (e.g. "Inglês: avançado"). Use Brazilian Portuguese vocabulary and keep technology names in English.

## Latin America (general Spanish-speaking markets)
<!-- tags: es-419, latam, latin america, colombia, argentina, chile, peru, spanish -->
Across Colombia, Argentina, Chile and Peru conventions are close to Mexico: "hoja de vida" is the usual name in Colombia, "currículum" elsewhere. One to two pages, reverse-chronological, explicit language levels, formal register. Use neutral Latin American Spanish ("computadora", "celular") unless a specific country is targeted. Photos and personal data are optional and should never be invented.
