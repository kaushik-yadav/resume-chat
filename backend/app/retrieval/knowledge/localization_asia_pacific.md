# Hiring conventions: Asia-Pacific

## India (en-IN)
<!-- tags: en-IN, india, indian -->
Document name: "resume" or "CV". One to two pages. A short career objective or summary is common. Personal details (date of birth, marital status, father's name) appear on traditional resumes but are increasingly omitted, especially at multinational and tech companies - do not add them. Notice period may be mentioned when relevant; current or expected CTC (salary) does not belong on the resume. British-leaning spelling is common (organisation), though American spelling is accepted.

## Japan (ja-JP)
<!-- tags: ja-JP, japan, japanese, rirekisho -->
Japanese companies commonly expect two documents: the "rirekisho" (履歴書), a standardized form with a photo, contact details, education and work history, and the "shokumu keirekisho" (職務経歴書), a free-form description of work experience and achievements. Foreign-affiliated companies in Japan often accept an English resume. Use polite, formal Japanese (keigo-appropriate phrasing). When translating, explain that the rirekisho form itself must be filled in separately; produce the content for the shokumu keirekisho.

## Singapore (en-SG)
<!-- tags: en-SG, singapore -->
Close to UK/US conventions: two pages, no photo required, reverse-chronological. Candidates sometimes state nationality or work-pass status because it affects eligibility; include it only if the candidate provided it.

## Australia (en-AU)
<!-- tags: en-AU, australia, new zealand, en-NZ -->
Two to three pages is acceptable. No photo, no date of birth. Australian/British spelling (organisation, optimise). Referees are often listed at the end or marked "available on request".
